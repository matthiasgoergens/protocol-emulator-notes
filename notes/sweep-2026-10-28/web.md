## Five most actionable lessons

1. Treat "novel approaches to design and verification methodologies" as a scoring axis: put a short, concrete "how we verified" section at the front of the submission, not buried. SymbiYosys + cocotb is the common baseline across about 15 repos and will not differentiate.
2. Show that the checks can fail: mutation kill rate with explained survivors, negative controls per property, tool-removal gates (as mrnathanhumphrey does), because Jane Street's results post praised testing tools "against inputs that should fail" and warned about building tools faster than you can check them.
3. Cross-check in an independent domain or tool pair (two simulators, RTL vs gate-level netlist, SPICE-level or FPGA run), mirroring David Garner's SPICE check which Jane Street singled out. Include a gate-level simulation of the post-PnR netlist.
4. Make the artefact easy for the next person to follow: Jane Street rewarded write-ups detailed "for the next person to follow along" and visual walkthroughs; plan an interactive or waveform-driven walkthrough, and record run commands and results (also needed for their open-source requirement).
5. Try Harmonic Aristotle (or Lean + Aristotle) on one or two core invariants soon and record the outcome either way; it launched yesterday, Jane Street names AI-assisted verification, and nobody public has claimed it for this contest yet. Keep it as an extra layer, not the only evidence, since its Verilog capability is unmeasured.

Also: plan for 6x4 and re-check the Jane Street post for the 8x4 and final-submission-form updates (none as of 2026-10-28); Jane Street emails sign-ups, so check the inbox as well.
