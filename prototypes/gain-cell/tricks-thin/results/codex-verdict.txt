## Load-bearing refutations

1. **The 1.21 ms / 137 µs calculation uses mismatched read conditions.**

   Read behaviour depends on `VLO`: [cell.py:207](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/cell.py:207) drives WBL to `VLO` throughout the read preparation. Nevertheless, [matrix.py:25](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/matrix.py:25) indexes read files only by `VBAR`, then reuses those thresholds for every VLO at [matrix.py:66](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/matrix.py:66).

   The actual header of [read-nlv-bar0.1.txt:1](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/results/read-nlv-bar0.1.txt:1) says `VLO=0.1`, whereas the headline hold files use `VLO=0.3`. Thus the claimed 0.30/0.10 V cell was judged using thresholds from a 0.10/0.10 V read circuit.

   There is a VLO=0.3/VBAR=0.1 read file, but [read-nlv-bar0.1-rbar0.txt:1](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/results/read-nlv-bar0.1-rbar0.txt:1) pulls the selected bar to 0 V during read, so it is not the fixed-0.1 V configuration either.

   Consequently, no saved read sweep validates either 1.21 ms or 137 µs for the claimed circuit. They might remain close, but that requires a matching read run.

2. **“Every unselected write transistor has \(V_{GS}\le -VLO\)” is false in the simulated cell.**

   [cell.py:73](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/cell.py:73) connects the write NMOS as D=WBL, G=WWL, S=SN. Therefore its model \(V_{GS}\) is \(V_{WWL}-V_{SN}\), not \(V_{WWL}-V_{WBL}\).

   In the headline parked case, [hold-lv-vlo0.3-bar0.1-park-v2.txt:10](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/results/hold-lv-vlo0.3-bar0.1-park-v2.txt:10) has the ff/85 °C stored zero at 0.195 V after writing and below 0.3 V throughout the 50 ms run. Its write transistor therefore begins around \(V_{GS}=-0.195\) V, not \(\le-0.3\) V. Other corners show the same counterexample.

   Parking WBL high can still reduce leakage, but the blanket \(V_{GS}\) explanation is wrong for stored-zero cells.

3. **The gate-drain-tunnelling attribution characterises the opposite D/S orientation.**

   The real cell uses D=WBL and S=SN at [cell.py:73](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/cell.py:73). In contrast, the leakage experiments explicitly use D=SN and S=WBL at [leakdc.py:14](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/leakdc.py:14) and [leakterm.py:28](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/leakterm.py:28).

   That may preserve total leakage if the compact model is perfectly symmetric, but it does not establish that the actual cell’s limiting current is specifically gate-*drain* overlap tunnelling. Moreover, the full transient was not decomposed: [leakdc.txt:7](/home/matthias/prog/janestreet/emulator-wt-gc-thin/prototypes/gain-cell/tricks-thin/results/leakdc.txt:7) shows substantial storage-transistor gate current as well. “The remaining floor is the write gate” is therefore unsupported.
